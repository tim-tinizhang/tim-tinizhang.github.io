Works in Progress

When Problems Abroad Hit Home: Anti-Elite Frames and Domestic Political Beliefs in China
Independent Research
Abstract
Authoritarian governments often amplify failures abroad, expecting unfavorable comparisons to bolster domestic legitimacy. Yet narratives about foreign problems may carry causal claims that travel beyond their original targets. This ongoing project develops a theory of diagnostic portability: when negative news about a foreign country attributes hardship to elite capture rather than administrative or market failure, audiences may apply the same diagnosis to their own society. The argument will be tested through a preregistered three-arm survey experiment in China. The core comparison holds the foreign event, factual content, source, and negative valence constant while varying its causal frame. The study examines whether anti-elite framing can simultaneously worsen evaluations of the foreign target and strengthen beliefs that domestic institutions favor privileged groups, whether it attenuates the comparative-legitimation effect of foreign bad news, and whether these effects are stronger among economically insecure respondents. The project links authoritarian information management to the cross-border movement of anti-elite ideas and identifies a potential limit of comparative propaganda: governments may shape exposure more readily than interpretation.
Tracking Bill Changes in China’s Two-phase Lawmaking Process
With Jiying Jiang
Abstract
Why do some bills bypass the public consultation stage? How do legal provisions change under the two-phase legislative procedure? What roles do different issue areas and stakeholders play in this process? In this co-authored, ongoing project, we construct a dataset covering 486 laws promulgated by the 11th through 14th National People’s Congresses of China, 381 public consultation records, and the draft texts at each stage of the two-phase legislative process for 98 of these laws, drawing on an efficient, AI-assisted data workflow that I develop. Using automated text analysis in R, we currently employ cosine similarity, Jaro–Winkler string distance, and related methods to match provisions and trace textual changes at the paragraph (kuan) level.
